# PortTrip Blog Tone & Style Guide

## The Reader

Our reader is a cruiser who has paid good money for their holiday and wants to squeeze maximum value from every port stop. They're not a backpacker or budget traveler — they're someone who chose a cruise for comfort and convenience, but is wondering if they can do better than the €150 ship excursion. They're often mildly anxious about logistics (missing the ship, getting lost, wasting time) and need confidence, not more complexity.

**Write for them, not for the algorithm.**

---

## Voice by Blog Type

| Blog Type | Voice |
|-----------|-------|
| Comparison | Analytical and fair — state your verdict upfront |
| Listicle | Helpful curator — specific and filtered, not encyclopedic |
| Tutorial | Patient teacher — use "we", share real experience |
| How-to | Practical problem-solver — direct, no fluff |
| Review | Honest reviewer — admit drawbacks to earn trust |
| Thought leadership | Opinionated expert — take a stand, back it with evidence |

## Universal Style Rules

**Be specific. Never vague.**
- ✓ "Add 90 minutes of buffer before all-aboard — we've seen cruisers cut it to 15 minutes and stress the entire way back"
- ✗ "Make sure you have enough time to return to the ship"

**Lead with the conclusion.**
Cruisers scan. Put the answer in the first paragraph. Never tease it for 1,000 words.

**Short paragraphs.** 2-3 sentences max. Single sentence is fine.

**No filler openings.** Never start with "In today's world..." or "Have you ever wondered..." Get to the point.

**No hype words.** Banned: revolutionary, game-changing, amazing, incredible, ultimate, best-ever. Use specifics instead.

**No passive voice.** "We tested it" not "It was tested."

**Numbers are concrete.** "€85" beats "significant savings." "30 seconds" beats "quickly."

## Mandatory E-E-A-T Requirements

**Every blog post MUST include at least one of the following:**

1. **A metric** — "4.9★ from 2,400+ cruisers" / "€85 avg saved per port" / "50+ ports covered" / "plan in 30 seconds"
2. **A testimonial or community quote** — real feedback from a cruiser
3. **A personal/team experience** — "When we tested this in Santorini..." / "Our team found..."
4. **A review link** — link to trust signals once available (Trustpilot, Product Hunt, etc.)

**If none of these can be included, do not publish the post.** Ask the user to provide one before publishing.

---

## Competitor Mentions: The Rules

1. **Lead with their strengths.** If mentioning ChatGPT, acknowledge it's good at general itinerary suggestions before explaining what it misses.
2. **Be specific about gaps.** Don't say "it's not built for cruise travelers." Say "ChatGPT doesn't know your all-aboard time is 17:00, so its suggestions don't account for your 90-minute return window."
3. **Cite confidence levels.** If competitor data isn't verified, add: *"Based on publicly available information — check [competitor] for current features."*
4. **Never mock or trash-talk.** We win by being more useful, not by tearing down others.

## Unique Angle: Non-Negotiable

Every blog post must have a clearly stated unique angle. "More comprehensive" is NOT an angle.

**Valid angles:**
- "The only guide written from the port gate, not the city center — with real timing from dock to each sight"
- "Honest test of ChatGPT for cruise planning — we tried it on 5 actual ports before writing this"
- "Covers the tender delay problem that every other planning guide ignores"

**Invalid angles:**
- "Comprehensive guide"
- "Everything you need to know"
- "Better version of what already ranks"

## CTA Rules

- **One CTA per post.** At the end. Never in the middle.
- **Soft, not salesy.** "Try PortTrip free" — not "Sign up now and unlock all features!"
- **Contextual.** The CTA should feel like a natural next step, not an ad.
- **Standard CTA:** "Try PortTrip free for your first 3 ports — no credit card needed." with link to https://porttrip.app

---

## Internal Linking Rules

- Every cluster page must link to its pillar. This is non-negotiable.
- Pillar pages must link to all published cluster pages.
- Aim for 2-4 internal links per post, minimum.
- Vary anchor text — don't repeat the same phrase twice in one post.
- Links should be contextual — the surrounding sentence should make the link feel helpful, not inserted.

---

## FAQ Sections

- Always pull questions from real People Also Ask data provided for that keyword.
- Answer in 2-4 sentences. Direct answers, not teases.
- If no PAA data provided, ask the user before inventing questions.

---

## What We Never Do

- Fabricate features or capabilities not in features.yaml
- Make competitor claims without confidence data
- Use web search to substitute for real SERP research
- Publish without human review
- Write "another version of what already ranks" — if the unique angle isn't clear, don't write the post
- Auto-approve for publication — always save as draft: true first
